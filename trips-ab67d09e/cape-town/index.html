Why Constantia for the base?

It's the middle of the peninsula: 13 min to Kirstenbosch and Hout Bay, 16 to Muizenberg, about 30 to Boulders, the Waterfront and Imhoff Farm. Being out of the city means more space, a private pool and calmer evenings, and you're on the sheltered side of the mountain when the south-easter blows. Camps Bay and Llandudno are prettier but cost far more over New Year and are windier. Want more village and more view? See option B.

Driving it

The longest drives are Constantia → Aquila (about 2½ h) and the coast road back from Stanford (about 2½ h with stops). Every other move is under 2 hours. Times on the map are from OpenStreetMap routing without traffic, so add 20–30% in holiday traffic. Drive on the left, and avoid driving after dark on rural roads.

Sadaf's pick

Coot Club: lagoon, mountains and a treehouse

Coot Club is a small nature lodge on 465 hectares of private reserve on the Klein River lagoon near Stanford, about 2 hours from Cape Town and 20 minutes from Hermanus. Architect-built boathouses face across the water to the Maanschynkop mountains, and BirdLife South Africa helps manage the land. It's the calm, lake-and-mountain heart of the trip.

3 nights · Tue 12 – Fri 15 Jan

Why it works with a 6- and 7-year-old

  • Children of all ages welcome. Under-3s stay free, and ages 3–18 are charged as extra children. Childminding can be booked.
  • Included: breakfast and dinner, kayaks, paddle boards (with life jackets and wetsuits), fat bikes for adults and kids, archery, lawn games, two pools, a wood-fired hot tub, and kids' extras: slip-and-slide, a treehouse with a daily tea party, and an indoor play and cinema room.
  • Extras: 4x4 beach expeditions and sandboarding in Walker Bay, quad bikes, tubing and horse riding.
  • Rooms for 4: the Starboard Boathouse (master + twin room, sleeps 2 adults + 2 children; one unit) or a Port Boathouse (three bedrooms; three units).

Day by day

Twenty-one days at a child's pace

One main outing a day, pool or beach every afternoon, and early starts while it's cool and still. Summer daylight in Cape Town runs about 05:40 to 20:00 (calculated for the trip dates), so long, slow evenings come free.

Day1
Mon 28 DecIn the air

Overnight to the Cape

A normal day at home, early tea, pyjamas in the hand luggage. Taxi to Heathrow for VS 414, 21:10 → Cape Town 10:45 the next morning (11 h 35, Boeing 787). The children sleep through most of it, and Cape Town is only 2 hours ahead of London in January, so there's no real jet lag.

Fill in the four SARS online traveller declarations (free) a few days before you fly.

Day2
Tue 29 DecConstantia · night 1

Hello, Cape Town

Land 10:45. Passports, bags, then collect the SUV and both booster seats at the airport. Drive to the villa in Constantia (24 km, about 28 min). Food shop, swim, early night.

Day one in strong sun: SPF 50 and hats on before anyone goes in the pool.

Day3
Wed 30 DecConstantia · night 2

Up Table Mountain

Take the first cableway cars (book online the day before and check the live status; it shuts in high wind). Rotating cabin, dassies on the summit, easy short paths. Then lunch and rock pools at Camps Bay. The Atlantic side is cold, so it's for paddling rather than swimming.

Mornings are usually calmer and clearer. Leave the top before the 'tablecloth' cloud rolls over.

Day4
Thu 31 DecConstantia · night 3

New Year's Eve at Kirstenbosch

Slow morning by the pool. If Kirstenbosch repeats its New Year's Eve Summer Sunset Concert (it held one in 2025, starring Mango Groove), take a picnic and blankets onto the lawns under the mountain. Under-6s are free, and you're home in time for a family 'midnight' at 21:00. Parents can toast the real one by the pool.

The 2026-27 line-up isn't out yet. Tickets sell fast once it's announced (Webtickets is the only seller).

Day5
Fri 1 JanConstantia · night 4

Camels and ponies at Imhoff Farm

Imhoff Farm, Kommetjie (29 km, about 31 min): Higgeldy Piggeldy farmyard, camel rides, a 15-minute pony ride, a snake park and good farm cafés. Afternoon on long, empty Noordhoek beach or a nap.

New Year's Day is a public holiday and the beaches get very busy. Check Imhoff's holiday hours.

Day6
Sat 2 JanConstantia · night 5

To the end of Africa

Cape Point (50 km, about 59 min): the Flying Dutchman funicular up to the old lighthouse, the Cape of Good Hope sign, ostriches by the road. Lunch in Simon's Town and home by mid-afternoon.

Baboons: keep food out of sight and car windows shut. Never feed them.

Day7
Sun 3 JanConstantia · night 6

First surf lesson

Muizenberg (13 km, about 16 min): the painted beach huts, gentle False Bay waves and warmer water (about 19 °C on average). A private kids' surf lesson each, wetsuits included, then fish and chips in Kalk Bay harbour.

Swim between the lifeguard flags. Muizenberg has Shark Spotters, so learn the flag colours.

Day8
Mon 4 JanConstantia · night 7

Aquarium and Waterfront

Two Oceans Aquarium at the V&A Waterfront (21 km, about 24 min): the kelp forest, the penguins and the touch pools. Then boats, seals in the harbour, street performers and ice cream.

Go early on a weekday. It gets busy in the holidays.

Day9
Tue 5 JanConstantia · night 8

Seals and Chapman's Peak

Hout Bay (11 km, about 13 min): a 45-minute boat trip to Seal Island. Then drive Chapman's Peak (toll R68 each way) to Noordhoek for a sunset picnic.

Look at the seals and don't touch them. Seal rabies is present on this coast (see Health).

Day10
Wed 6 JanConstantia · night 9

Kirstenbosch and the treetop walk

Kirstenbosch (9 km, about 13 min): the 'Boomslang' treetop walkway, the fynbos and proteas, and a picnic on the lawns. Afternoon: second surf lesson or the pool. Pack for the safari.

This is the day school goes back. The trip misses about 8 school days (see the note at the top).

Day11
Thu 7 JanAquila · night 10

Into the Karoo: safari

Leave by 09:00 for Aquila Private Game Reserve (195 km, about 2 h 28, via the Huguenot Tunnel). Lunch from 12:00, check in at 14:00, then the afternoon game drive looking for lion, elephant, rhino and buffalo. Marshmallows and stargazing after dinner.

Bring each child's passport. Aquila checks children's ID at check-in.

Day12
Fri 8 JanAquila · night 11

Junior rangers

Early-morning game drive, then a big breakfast. The kids join the Junior Ranger programme (ages 12 and under, extra cost) and the adventure play zone while you sit by the infinity pool. Afternoon drive.

The Karoo is hot in the middle of the day (30 °C or more) and cold at dawn, so bring fleeces for the open vehicles.

Day13
Sat 9 JanFranschhoek · night 12

Goats and the Winelands

Morning drive and breakfast, then down to the Winelands (Aquila → Franschhoek 151 km, about 2 h 00). On the way, stop at Fairview near Paarl to see the goat tower and do the kids' tasting. Check in to Franschhoek.

The farm stalls on the R45 are good for lunch.

Day14
Sun 10 JanFranschhoek · night 13

Babylonstoren gardens

Babylonstoren's huge fruit and vegetable garden (children under 18 free): a prickly-pear maze, chickens, and fruit picked straight from the trees. Lunch at the Greenhouse, then swimming.

Book a Babylonstoren lunch well ahead. It's peak season.

Day15
Mon 11 JanFranschhoek · night 14

Spier: the family wine farm

Spier, Stellenbosch (45 km, about 48 min): picnics under the oaks, the play garden, and Eagle Encounters (birds of prey) on the farm. Or an easy day in Franschhoek village instead.

If you're doing a wine tasting, have one non-drinking driver. South Africa's limit is 0.05%.

Day16
Tue 12 JanCoot Club · night 15

Over the pass to the lagoon

Take the Franschhoek Pass past Theewaterskloof Dam and through the apple country to Coot Club on the Klein River lagoon near Stanford (about 1 h 40). Kayaks, paddle boards and the treehouse are all yours.

The last stretch to Coot Club is on a gravel road. Take it slowly.

Day17
Wed 13 JanCoot Club · night 16

Lagoon day

Morning yoga or coffee on the deck. Paddle on flat, warm-ish lagoon water, fat-bike rides, archery, the slip-and-slide, and the kids' tea party in the treehouse. Wood-fired hot tub at sunset.

Breakfast and dinner are included at Coot Club. Lunch is à la carte.

Day18
Thu 14 JanCoot Club · night 17

Dunes and Hermanus

Coot Club's 4x4 beach expedition into Walker Bay Nature Reserve (dune climbing and sandboarding, extra cost), or 20 minutes into Hermanus for the cliff path and rock pools. Whale season is over by January (it runs roughly June to November), so don't promise whales.

Stanford village has a good Saturday market and ice-cream shops.

Day19
Fri 15 JanKalk Bay · night 18

The coast road back

The slow way back: the R44 along Clarence Drive (Kogel Bay, Betty's Bay), stopping at Stony Point for a second penguin colony. Then round False Bay to Kalk Bay for the last night (about 2 h 30 driving in total).

Kalk Bay harbour at sunset: fishing boats coming in, and seals waiting for scraps (keep the kids back).

Day20
Sat 16 JanIn the air

Penguins goodbye, fly home

Boulders Beach at 08:00 opening, before the crowds: African penguins on the boardwalks and in the sea. Last swim at Muizenberg, late lunch, then to the airport (38 km, about 48 min). Return the car about 18:00 for VS 479 21:00 → LHR 06:50.

Allow extra time for traffic on the M3/N2 on a Saturday afternoon in the holidays.

Day21
Sun 17 JanHome

Home to Wimbledon

Land at Heathrow 06:50. Home to SW20 for a long breakfast. Two hours' time difference means everyone is back to normal by Monday.

Pack a warm layer in the hand luggage: London in January after 27 °C is a shock.

Alternative base: scenic village option

Option B: a sea-view village instead of Constantia

Option A is the Constantia pool villa above: central, private and leafy, but suburban. Option B swaps it for a small False Bay fishing village with sea and mountain views, tidal pools and cafés you can walk to, and the warmer side of the peninsula for swimming. The price is longer drives into the city. All three homes below showed as available for the exact dates (29 Dec – 7 Jan, 9 nights, 2 adults + 2 children) on Airbnb on 11 Oct 2026. Prices are Airbnb totals including fees.

Our pick for option B

Kalk Bay: 4 Dalebrook Place

The best match for "more scenic, smaller-town, better views". It's a well-reviewed sea-view flat in the liveliest little village on the peninsula, across the road from a tidal pool, with Muizenberg's surf school 4 minutes away and Boulders 14. It's also £3,753 cheaper than Mountain Magic, though £1,226 more than the Bergvliet saver house. The trade-offs are 2 bedrooms (the children share), no private pool, and a busy road and railway to cross with the kids. If you'd rather have space, take the Simon's Town house by Boulders. If you want a pool and wild views, take Scarborough, but it's remote.

Safari

Big Five in the Karoo, with no malaria

Kruger is a flight away and needs antimalarials in summer. Aquila Private Game Reserve at Touws River is about 2½ hours' drive from the base, malaria-free, and set up for young families.

Aquila's child policy (checked 11 Oct 2026)

  • No minimum age for the lodge or the traditional 4x4 game drives. Children must be with an adult.
  • Children 4–11 pay 50% of the adult rate, and 12+ pay the full rate. Family Cottages take 2 adults + 2 children, with the kids in a loft.
  • For kids: an outdoor adventure zone, an indoor play area, mini golf, giant chess and the Junior Ranger programme (extra cost). Childminding 09:00–21:00 costs extra.
  • Horse riding, quad bikes and the spa are 16+ only. Pools must be supervised by an adult.
  • Bring the children's passports or birth certificates, which are checked at reception.

To be honest about it: Aquila is about 10,000 ha of restored Karoo. It's a fenced private reserve, much smaller than Kruger (about 2 million ha), and the lions are kept in a separate camp. Sightings are almost guaranteed, but it feels more managed and less wild than a Kruger or Botswana safari. For a 6- and 7-year-old's first safari it's ideal. Inverdoorn (same group) isn't a good fit: it has no children's facilities and its terms say under-12s can't join the scheduled game drives (private vehicle only) and it suggests families go to Aquila. Sanbona is about 3½ hours away, beyond our limit.

Practicalities

Before you go, and while you're there

Entry

British and Danish passports: visa-free for up to 90 days as tourists. Passports must be valid for at least 30 days after you leave South Africa and have 2 blank pages (GOV.UK). From 1 July 2026 every traveller, children included, must submit a free SARS online traveller declaration (SATMS) before flying, on the SARS website or app.

Children's documents

Current rule (Home Affairs, since Nov 2019): foreign, visa-exempt children travelling with both parents need only a valid passport. The birth-certificate requirement now applies to South African children. Lily's Danish passport is fine. Our advice is still to carry copies of both children's full birth certificates showing both parents, because of the mixed nationalities, because officials or airlines can ask, and because Aquila asks for ID at check-in. If one parent ever travels alone with the kids, take a consent affidavit as well.

Health

Cape Town and the whole Western Cape are malaria-free (TravelHealthPro: no risk outside the far north-east). No yellow fever certificate is needed coming from the UK. Have routine jabs up to date. TravelHealthPro recommends hepatitis A, tetanus and typhoid for most travellers, so see the GP or travel nurse 4–6 weeks before. Rabies is now present in Cape fur seals: never touch or feed seals, and get urgent care after any bite or scratch. Ask about pre-exposure rabies jabs because of the surf lessons.

January weather

Hot and dry: about 27 °C by day, 16 °C at night, and roughly 15 mm of rain for the month. The Cape Doctor south-easter can blow hard for days, closing the cableway and sandblasting beaches. Pick sheltered beaches (Boulders, St James, Clifton) on windy days. UV is extreme, so cover up from 11:00 to 15:00. The Karoo hits 30 °C or more.

Sea & water safety

Atlantic side (Camps Bay, Llandudno, Kommetjie): cold, often 12–16 °C, and colder with upwelling, so good for paddling and rock pools. False Bay (Muizenberg, St James, Boulders): warmer, about 19 °C on average, and the place for swimming and surfing. Swim between lifeguard flags. Rip currents are common. Learn the Shark Spotters flags at Muizenberg. Don't swim in rivers or dams (bilharzia risk, TravelHealthPro).

Staying safe

Most visits are trouble-free, but crime is real. Keep phones out of sight, nothing visible in the car, doors locked at traffic lights, and park in guarded spots (tip the car guards). Don't drive after dark on rural roads or stop on the N2 near the airport. Hike only on busy routes, in groups, and tell someone your plan. Baboons at Cape Point will steal food.

Power & load-shedding

Plugs are Type M (three large round pins), with newer sockets Type N and two-pin Type C also common. Bring 2–3 adapters. Load-shedding: none nationally for more than 500 days. On 9 Oct 2026 Eskom reported 511 days, with only targeted "load reduction" in parts of Gauteng and KwaZulu-Natal. Keep a power bank anyway.

Driving

Drive on the left, as at home. UK photocard licences are accepted, and an International Driving Permit is a cheap backup. Speed limits are 60 / 100 / 120 km/h. Lots of fuel stations are attended, so tip the attendant R5–R10. Card payments work almost everywhere. Keep a few R10/R20 notes for car guards.

Time & money

SAST = GMT+2 all year, no daylight saving. In January that's 2 hours ahead of London, which is why there's no jet lag. Currency is the rand: about R21.87 to £1 (11 Oct 2026). Water from Cape Town's taps is safe to drink.
